Lanyards

Energy-absorbing lanyards in single- and twin-leg versions, including 12 ft free-fall models for foot-level tie-off, plus positioning and restraint lanyards. Check the free-fall rating and the clearance the maker requires.

Safety note. Reference only — not training and not a substitute for the manufacturer’s instructions, a competent person, or OSHA/ANSI requirements. Inspect gear before each use, remove from service any equipment that has arrested a fall, and verify specs with the manufacturer.

An energy-absorbing lanyard has a fixed length and a built-in absorber, and under Z359.13 it is rated for either a 6 ft or a 12 ft free fall. The 12 ft versions are for tie-off down to foot level and stretch more, so they need more clearance below. OSHA’s 1926.502(d) caps free fall at 6 ft and arresting force at 1,800 lb, and 1910.140 allows more only if the maker designed and tested for it. Where headroom is tight, compare an SRL, and always use the maker’s clearance chart (see Fall clearance).
