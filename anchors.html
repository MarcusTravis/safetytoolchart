Anchors

Anchorage connectors that give a harness system something rated to tie to: beam clamps, cross-arm straps, roof anchors, concrete and bolt-on anchors. The structure they attach to must carry the load too.

Data last checked: Nov 25, 2026 · What changed

Safety note. Reference only — not training and not a substitute for the manufacturer’s instructions, a competent person, or OSHA/ANSI requirements. Inspect gear before each use, remove from service any equipment that has arrested a fall, and verify specs with the manufacturer.

The anchorage is what the whole system ties to. Under 1926.502(d) it must support at least 5,000 lb per worker attached, or be designed, installed and used as part of a complete system with a safety factor of at least two, under a qualified person. A beam clamp or strap is only as good as the structure it grips, so the structure has to carry the load too. Where the anchor sits also decides swing-fall risk (see Swing falls).
