    Why this exists

    Falls are consistently among the leading causes of death in U.S. construction, and OSHA’s fall protection rule is regularly one of its most-cited standards. Picking gear still means opening one brand site at a time. This chart lines up the specs that matter — class, capacity, length, connectors, gate strength — with a link to the maker’s own page for each.

    How to use it

    Search above, or open a catalog. Each catalog is a dense, sortable chart with filter chips for brand, type, ANSI class, CSA listing and capacity. Tick Compare on up to four rows to line them up. New to the standards? Read Basics & standards first.

    About this site

    Safety Tool Chart is free and has no ads, no affiliate links, and no tracking parameters in its links. Facts come from official manufacturer pages, spec sheets and instruction manuals, and from public retailer listings for the exact part number, last checked Nov 16, 2026. Every row links the page its specs came from. Notes are written in original words; marketing text, photos and tables are never copied.

    Price rule. Price = lowest in-stock price for a new unit of that exact part number seen on a public US retailer or manufacturer-store listing (seller named and linked), checked on the date shown. No affiliate links. If no live price was found, a $ band is shown as an editorial estimate. If no live price was seen for that exact part number, the cell shows an editorial band, labelled “Editorial estimate”: $ under $100 · $$ $100–$250 · $$$ $250–$600 · $$$$ $600–$1,500 · $$$$$ over $1,500.

    Logos. Official brand marks are shown only where the maker’s own site offers one (sources in icons/README.md); other brands appear as a plain text badge. Marks belong to their owners.
